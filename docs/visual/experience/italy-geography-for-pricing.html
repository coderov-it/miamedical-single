What a comune actually is — and why it is safe to price on

I said "municipality" earlier and that was a bad word for it, because it points your mind at towns. A comune is not a town or a city area. It is the one unit Italy divides all of its land into — cities, towns, villages, farmland, mountains, coastline. Every square metre of Italy sits inside exactly one comune. Nothing is left over.

The four levels, from biggest to smallest

Why "Pourashava" threw you off

Bangladesh uses different kinds of unit depending on whether land is rural or urban: Union Parishad for villages, Pourashava for towns, City Corporation for big cities. So "Pourashava" correctly means an urban area to you.

Italy has no such split. One type — the comune — is used everywhere, for the largest city and the emptiest mountainside alike. So the closest equivalent is not Pourashava on its own; it is Union Parishad, Pourashava and City Corporation all merged into a single type.

This is the part that answers your worry

"We need to cover the whole of Italy, villages and towns everywhere" — you already do. The ~7,900 comuni are a complete partition of the country: no gaps, no overlaps, nothing outside. A village is inside a comune, so it inherits that comune's fee automatically and needs no row of its own. You never have to list villages.

And the CAP? That is just a post-office number

The CAP (Codice di Avviamento Postale) is a postal code, exactly like the 4-digit codes in Bangladesh. It is drawn by the post office, for sorting mail efficiently — not by the government, and not to mark any boundary anyone governs.

That is the whole reason it does not line up with comuni. It was never trying to. Sometimes a CAP is smaller than a comune, sometimes bigger — and there is no rule telling you which:

So the rule is: comune decides, CAP may only refine

The lookup pins the comune first, then checks for an exception row keyed on the pair ISTAT:CAP. A CAP shared between comuni then cannot leak one comune's price onto its neighbours, and a big city can still price its coastal district apart from its centre. Both directions of mismatch, one rule.

What this means for the sheet your client gave you

Two things worth being concrete about, because they shrink the job:

7,900 Rows for all of Italy The absolute maximum, if your client ever delivered everywhere. It is a small table, and the ISTAT list is a free one-time import refreshed maybe yearly.
~50–200 Rows you will actually have The comuni your client really serves. Everything else routes to a phone quote — which is what they do today anyway.
0 Villages to list A frazione inherits its comune's fee. You never enumerate villages, and you never have a gap in coverage.

The one question left for your client

Are their spreadsheet rows comuni (town names like Fiumicino, Tivoli, Sacrofano) or provinces (the two-letter codes RM, FI)? Comuni means ~7,900 possible rows and fine pricing; provinces means ~107 and much coarser. It changes the import, so it is worth a direct question rather than a guess — and if the sheet turns out to mix both levels, the comune rows win and the province rows become the fallback for anything unlisted.
