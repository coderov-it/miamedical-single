The delivery fee comes out of the price list, not out of a calculation

The customer types an address, picks a suggestion, and the fee appears. Underneath there is no map and no distance maths — only the comune (municipality) and the CAP (postal code) that every Italian address already carries, looked up in your client's sheet.

Demo data. The fees, the comune list and the ISTAT codes are invented, to stand in for your client's spreadsheet. Type Portuense for a plainly covered comune, Romagnoli for a CAP priced apart from the city it sits in, and Vannucci for an address outside the sheet. Place names and the euro number format are the storefront's real ones; everything else on this page is English so it reads.

      Your client's sheet, as a table

      One row per comune, keyed on the ISTAT code. A CAP row beats its comune row — that is how Lido di Ostia sits inside the comune of Rome and still carries its own fee. The indented rows are those CAP exceptions.

      Why it is built this way

      There is no zone to detect

      No polygons, no point-in-polygon test. Autocomplete already hands back the comune and the CAP, so the fee is a lookup on an indexed column. The part you were worried about never has to be built.

      The fallback is a list, not a map

      Pricing needs one fact: the comune. A map makes the customer guess it by dropping a pin, and then we reverse-geocode the guess. A province → comune list makes them state it — no map tiles, no reverse geocoding, and no doubt about what we understood. Open "Can't find your address?" on the left to try it.

      CAP is not a unique key

      Rome has roughly 200 CAPs, and one CAP can span several small comuni. That is why the primary key is the comune's ISTAT code and the CAP is only an exception row. Do it the other way round and Rome becomes 200 rows that disagree with each other.

      Outside the list, nothing is guessed

      A comune that is not in the sheet routes to a phone quote rather than an automatic estimate — your client has to price those by hand anyway. Log the misses: the comuni people ask for repeatedly are the rows worth adding.

      The fee is computed server-side

      The browser sends an address; the server does the lookup and stores the figure on the order. A fee that arrives from the browser is a price the customer can edit.

      Open question for your client

      Does a sheet price cover delivery and collection? This is a rental — the van makes two trips. If those numbers are one-way, every quote on the site is half the real cost. Worth asking before the sheet gets imported.
